01 / Build the mathematical bridge

Densities are geometric.
So is their evolution.

The manuscript begins with tensors and differential forms, develops smooth manifolds, Stokes’ theorem and cohomology, then connects those tools to continuity equations and continuous normalizing flows.

A / LOCAL ALGEBRA

Forms and change of variables

Exterior powers encode oriented volume. Pullbacks and Jacobian determinants describe how integration changes under a smooth map—the bookkeeping behind density transport.

C / GENERATIVE DYNAMICS

A learned vector field

A continuous normalizing flow pushes a base probability law through an ODE. Flow matching learns a marginal velocity associated with a chosen probability path.

CONTINUITYCNFFLOW MATCHING
dx/dt = vₜ(x)   ·   ∂ₜρₜ + div(ρₜvₜ) = 0   ·   d/dt log ρₜ(xₜ) = −div vₜ(xₜ)
The last identity follows along a smooth characteristic with positive density. A global flow also needs appropriate regularity, existence and boundary or decay conditions; drawing arrows does not establish these hypotheses.

03 / The manuscript’s application proposal
Energy, pathways
and sequence design.
The applications part describes temperature-conditioned iterated energy-based flow matching with a learned energy/force oracle, followed by endpoint-constrained path generation and sequence design. This repository supplies the manuscript, not an implementation or trained model package.
01 / EQ
Condition on temperature.
An energy-defined target has density proportional to exp[−βE(x)]. Candidate reweighting estimates the marginal flow field without requiring reference simulation trajectories for each training target.
02 / TPS
Sample a constrained path.
Proposed path-space energies combine per-frame potential energies with a smoothness prior and fixed endpoints. Residue and side-chain masks specify which variables may change.
03 / SEQ
Propose compatible sequences.
A categorical flow can condition on endpoint and intermediate structural embeddings. Physics-based scoring, chemistry validity and independent sequence evaluation remain essential.
Scope matters. “Quantum-accuracy” here describes a learned surrogate of electronic-structure labels, not execution on a quantum computer. Finite importance sampling can have bias and weight degeneracy. Chirality, symmetry, constraints, oracle error and out-of-distribution behavior require explicit validation; a density objective alone does not remove them.
